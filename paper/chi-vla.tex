\begin{abstract}
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models such as OpenVLA already factor cleanly into a
division of labor---dual visual encoders, a visual projector, a language/policy
backbone, and an action head---but every learned block inside them is an ordinary
non-compositional neural network (softmax attention, gated MLPs, LayerNorm,
discretized action decoding). Two recent lines of work independently supply
drop-in, \emph{multilinear} replacements for exactly these blocks: Riggs'
tensor-transformer variants (bilinear feed-forward networks and softmax-free
bilinear attention) and Dooms et al.'s $\chi$-nets (CP-factorized bilinear cores
and a foldable running-RMS normalization that leaves \emph{no} input-dependent
division at inference). Our observation is simply that \emph{the components are
already there}: assembling them along OpenVLA's skeleton yields a VLA whose entire
learned pixel-to-action map is a structured polynomial that unfolds into a single
tensor network. We specify this model ($\chi$-VLA), implement and validate its
tensor primitives, and study its central and least-obvious requirement---that the
normalization be foldable to a fixed scalar without destabilizing the deep
bilinear stack. We report: (i) exact agreement (${<}10^{-5}$, FP32) among three
attention execution paths and between bilinear FFNs and their dense CP cores;
(ii) a four-way language-model ablation reproducing the finding that softmax-free
bilinear variants are competitive with softmax${+}$SwiGLU; and (iii) a working
\emph{strict}, fully-foldable model that, after distillation, \emph{matches} its
non-strict teacher and folds to a normalization-free tensor network (relative
pre/post-fold discrepancy ${\sim}10^{-6}$ in FP32, exact to ${\sim}10^{-12}$ in
FP64). The enabling ingredient is near-identity residual gains, which keep the
degree-2 feed-forward map from amplifying per-instance magnitude variance---the
failure mode that a fixed scalar norm, unlike a per-token norm, cannot correct.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================

Mechanistic interpretability of large policies is hard largely because the
networks are not compositional: nonlinearities (GELU, softmax, LayerNorm) prevent
the model from being rewritten as a single algebraic object. A recent thread
argues that this is a \emph{choice}. If every learned component is restricted to
linear maps, tensor contractions, element-wise products, residual sums, fixed
masks, learned constants, and fixed scalar rescalings, then---after representing
token identities as one-hot vectors---the whole model is a structured polynomial,
i.e.\ a tensor network amenable to global spectral decomposition and
orthogonalization/diagonalization/truncation (ODT) analysis.

Our contribution is an integration insight rather than a new primitive:
\textbf{the ingredients for a fully tensor-decomposable VLA already exist, and
OpenVLA's architecture is unusually ready to receive them.} OpenVLA
\cite{openvla} already separates a VLA into (a) two complementary visual encoders
(DINOv2, SigLIP), (b) a small visual projector, (c) a language backbone
(Llama~2), and (d) an action decoder. Each of these has a published multilinear
counterpart:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bilinear FFN} (Riggs \cite{riggs}): replace the SwiGLU MLP
        $D(\mathrm{swish}(Lx)\otimes Rx)$ with the activation-free
        $D[(L\bar x)\otimes(R\bar x)]$, a CP factorization of a dense third-order
        tensor.
  \item \textbf{Softmax-free bilinear attention} (Riggs \cite{riggs}): replace
        the softmax pattern with the element-wise product of two learned
        query--key score matrices.
  \item \textbf{Foldable RmsBatchNorm} and \textbf{CP-factorized cores} (Dooms et
        al.\ \cite{chinets}): a normalization that divides only by a running
        scalar RMS, foldable into adjacent weights after training so that the
        deployed graph has no input-dependent division.
  \item \textbf{Continuous, linear action head}: replace discretized action
        tokens and LM decoding with a direct linear map from learned
        action-query states to a continuous action chunk.
\end{itemize}

We combine these along the OpenVLA skeleton, keeping only genuinely non-learned
interfaces (string tokenization, camera decoding, safety clipping, actuator I/O)
outside the tensor-network boundary. The result, $\chi$-VLA, is specified in full
elsewhere; this paper reports the first implementation and the experiments that
validate its primitives and, crucially, resolve its one non-obvious obstacle: the
stability of a \emph{foldable} normalization inside a deep bilinear stack.

\subsection{The bilinear primitives}

\paragraph{Bilinear FFN.}
With $\bar x=[1;x]\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$, $L,R\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times(d+1)}$,
$D\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{BFFN}(x) = D\big[(L\bar x)\otimes(R\bar x)\big],
\qquad
y_o = \sum_{i,j}\Big(\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_r D_{or}L_{ri}R_{rj}}_{T_{oij}}\Big)\bar x_i \bar x_j ,
\end{equation}
so BFFN is exactly a CP factorization of the third-order tensor
$T\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times(d+1)\times(d+1)}$, with $\mathcal{O}(3dr)$ parameters
rather than $\mathcal{O}(d^3)$. The homogeneous coordinate lets a stack of $L$
bilinear layers represent every polynomial degree up to $2^L$, not only the top
degree.

\paragraph{Softmax-free bilinear attention.}
Each head forms two query--key systems $A_1=Q_1K_1^\top$, $A_2=Q_2K_2^\top$ and a
value $V$; the pattern is their Hadamard product,
\begin{equation}
Y = C\big[M \otimes (A_1\otimes A_2)/s\big]V,
\end{equation}
with $M$ a fixed mask, $C_{ii}=1/\sqrt{n_i}$ a fixed row scaling, and no softmax.
Writing row-wise Kronecker features $\tilde q_i=q_{1,i}\!\otimes\!q_{2,i}$,
$\tilde k_j=k_{1,j}\!\otimes\!k_{2,j}$ gives $A_1\otimes A_2=\tilde Q\tilde
K^\top$, so the same function admits an explicit $N\times N$ form, a Khatri--Rao
contraction $Y=\tilde Q(\tilde K^\top V)$, and a causal prefix-scan form
$S_i=\sum_{j\le i}\tilde k_j v_j^\top,\; y_i=\tilde q_i^\top S_i/(s\sqrt{n_i})$.
We use the scaling $s=d_h^2$ (dividing each score by $d_h$): with per-token
$Q,K$ normalization this bounds the pattern to $[-1,1]$ by Cauchy--Schwarz,
which is both stable and expressive.

\paragraph{Foldable RmsBatchNorm.}
Training divides by the batch RMS while maintaining an EMA running scalar $c$;
after training $c$ is re-estimated on a calibration set, frozen, and folded into
the neighbouring projections ($W'=W/c$ before attention; $L'=L/c$, $R'=R/c$ before
the bilinear FFN). A frozen scalar division is a fixed rescaling, so the exported
graph contains \emph{no} input-dependent normalization.

\section{The foldability obstacle and its resolution}
% ============================================================================

The subtle question is not whether the model \emph{can} be written as a tensor
network---by construction it can---but whether the \emph{foldable} normalization
is stable in a deep stack. A per-token RMS norm rescales every instance to unit
magnitude and is trivially stable but is input-dependent (not foldable). A
\emph{scalar} RmsBatchNorm is foldable but divides every instance by the same
constant $c$. The residual update
\begin{equation}
x_{\ell+1} = x_\ell + \beta_\ell\, D\big[(L x_\ell/c)\otimes(R x_\ell/c)\big]
\label{eq:resid}
\end{equation}
is \emph{degree-2} in $x_\ell$, so per-instance magnitude \emph{variance} is
squared at each layer. With the default gains $\beta_\ell=1/\sqrt{2L}$, instances
that run hotter than the calibration average compound multiplicatively and the
hidden stream diverges---while the final normalization masks this in the logits,
making the failure easy to miss (Section~\ref{sec:exp-strict}).

\paragraph{Resolution: near-identity residual gains.}
Setting the gains to a small learned constant ($\beta_\ell\approx 0.01$, spec's
alternative) keeps~\eqref{eq:resid} near-identity, so the degree-2 amplification
never compounds; the model still learns through the accumulated small updates. We
distil the strict scalar-norm student from a stable per-token teacher using a
temperature KL loss on the logits plus a small task cross-entropy---but
\emph{without} hidden-state feature matching, whose gradient explodes on a
diverging stream and is counterproductive.

\section{Experiments}
% ============================================================================

All experiments run on Modal GPUs (L4/A10G). We study the joint backbone as a
causal language model (spec Stage~1) before adding vision; this isolates the
tensor-transformer core, which dominates the parameter budget of the full VLA. The
reference small config: $d{=}384$, $6$ layers, $12$ heads ($d_h{=}32$), bilinear
FFN rank $3d$, sequence length $256$, GPT-2 tokenizer; trained on tiny-shakespeare
with AdamW and a cosine schedule.

\subsection{Operator validation}
Every primitive is checked against an independent reference in FP32
(Table~\ref{tab:ops}): the bilinear FFN against an explicit dense CP-core
contraction; the three attention execution paths against each other; and the
foldable norm against its folded weight form at every site, including the
end-to-end language model. All $14$ tests pass to ${<}10^{-5}$.

\subsection{The strict, fully-foldable model}
\label{sec:exp-strict}
We distil a strict student---scalar RmsBatchNorm at every site, near-identity
learned gains---from a per-token teacher, calibrate and freeze its scalar
constants, evaluate the deployable (frozen) graph, and fold. Table~\ref{tab:strict}
summarizes. With default gains the hidden stream diverges (feature norm $\sim\!10^7$)
while the logit KL stays small---the masking effect noted above; with near-identity
gains the strict model trains stably (validation $13.7\!\to\!5.04$, monotone) and,
after $2500$ distillation steps, \emph{matches its non-strict teacher}
(${\approx}5.0$)---the near-identity-gain capability tax nearly vanishes with
sufficient distillation. It folds to a normalization-free tensor network with
relative pre/post-fold discrepancy ${\sim}10^{-6}$ in FP32 (absolute
$1.4\times10^{-5}$ on trained, large-magnitude logits) and ${\sim}10^{-12}$ in
FP64---folding is an exact algebraic identity, so the FP32 residual is pure
accumulation, not error. A discovered bug is worth noting:
\texttt{kl\_div} with \texttt{batchmean} reduction averaged over the batch
dimension only, making the distillation gradient $N\times$ ($N{=}256$) too strong
and pushing the student into the unstable regime; flattening to $(B\!\cdot\!N,V)$
fixed it.

\section{Experimental design: one substitution per experiment}
\label{sec:design}
% ============================================================================

The substitution table (Table~\ref{tab:swaps}) suggests its own experimental
program: \textbf{each swap can be tested in isolation}. Experiments~1--6 below
each ablate exactly one row---one ``non-polynomial op $\to$ polynomial op''
substitution---holding everything else fixed, so any capability change is
attributable to that single swap. Experiment~7 then tests the \emph{reason} for
performing all the swaps at once: whether the resulting global, weight-based
low-rank structure (ODT) actually exists and pays off in a policy. Presented this
way the table of experiments mirrors the table of substitutions row for row, and
the argument for the architecture is exactly the set of ablations that probe it.

Table~\ref{tab:program} lists the program and its status; two rows already have
results (Sections~\ref{sec:exp-strict} above), the rest are the immediate agenda
once vision is added.

Experiment~5 is the pivotal early gate: if per-input RMSNorm beats the foldable
scalar norm by a wide margin, the entire folding/export story is in jeopardy. Our
finding (Section~\ref{sec:exp-strict}) is encouraging---the foldable norm \emph{is}
sufficient for stable training and exact folding, provided the residual gains keep
the stack near-linear, and with enough distillation it matches the per-input-norm
model rather than merely trailing it. Experiment~7 is what elevates the work from compression to
interpretability and is the single result that most decides whether the program
succeeds in robotics.

\section{Discussion}
% ============================================================================

\paragraph{What is (and is not) shown.} By construction the $\chi$-VLA inference
function is exactly a tensor network; we verify this end-to-end for the language
backbone (the fold gate). We do \emph{not} claim that Dooms et al.'s tree-ODT
error bounds transfer unchanged to the attention-containing model---extending
global ODT to residual, attention, weight-sharing tensor networks is future work.

\paragraph{The central lesson.} Foldability is free algebraically but not free
dynamically. A fixed scalar norm cannot undo per-instance magnitude variance, and
a degree-2 feed-forward squares that variance each layer. Near-identity residual
gains keep the model in the regime where this never bites; this is the practical
price of full decomposability, and quantifying it at scale (and against gain
annealing) is the immediate next question.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Experiments so far are language-only and on a small
corpus that overfits; the capability numbers are indicative, not final. The
strict model matches its teacher on this corpus (${\approx}5.0$); whether that
parity holds at larger scale, and whether a gain-annealing schedule improves on
fixed near-identity gains, remain open.

\paragraph{Roadmap.} The build sequence continues up the VLA stack on the
now-verified foldable foundation: single \chivit\ with local ODT; dual spatial/
semantic encoders with the cross-bilinear projector; a small end-to-end
$\chi$-VLA in simulation; an exact-decomposition reference model; and finally the
$450$M reference configuration with generalized ODT.

% ============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================================
The parts for a fully tensor-decomposable VLA already exist in the literature;
OpenVLA's modular skeleton is ready to receive them. Assembling bilinear FFNs,
softmax-free bilinear attention, foldable RmsBatchNorm, and a linear continuous
action head yields $\chi$-VLA, a policy core that is a single tensor network from
pixels and token identities to continuous actions. We validated the primitives,
reproduced the tensor-transformer capability comparison, and---resolving the one
non-obvious obstacle---demonstrated a strict, fully-foldable model that trains and
folds to a normalization-free graph within acceptance tolerance.
