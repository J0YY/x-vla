## Frozen factorial intervention

- Suite: LIBERO-Object.
- Checkpoints: the three immutable capable chi-ViT checkpoints, seeds 0, 1, and 2.
- States: official canonical episodes 20 through 29 for every task. These do not overlap the
  observation-shift program's episodes 0 through 9 or instruction-necessity and the abandoned old
  target-swap plan's episodes 40 through 49. They do overlap the broader failed local-rank state
  set, but no local score or outcome selects a state, target, condition, or gate here.
- Targets: all five co-present non-original objects for every official task. No target is selected
  or discarded.
- Rewrite: change exactly the BDDL language target, object of interest, and goal target. The
  simulator initialization, objects, regions, and all other BDDL syntax remain byte-identical.
- Horizon: full 280-step closed-loop evaluation with eight-action receding-horizon control.
- Precision: deterministic `highest` float32 matmul precision on NVIDIA RTX A6000.

Each checkpoint, task, episode, and co-present target forms one four-arm paired unit:

1. original BDDL goal with the original matching prompt,
2. original BDDL goal with the counterfactual mismatching prompt,
3. counterfactual BDDL goal with the counterfactual matching prompt,
4. counterfactual BDDL goal with the original mismatching prompt.

All four arms must begin from the exactly identical public physical simulator input, including the
image and robot state. The prescribed instruction and active success predicate differ according to
the factorial arm. Their execution order is a deterministic SHA-256-selected permutation fixed
without outcomes. The full matrix has 1,500 four-arm units and 6,000 rollouts.

## Frozen statistics and gates

The two directional paired advantages are original-goal matching minus mismatching success and
counterfactual-goal matching minus mismatching success. The symmetric aligned-prompt advantage is
the mean of those two differences.

All gates must pass:

- original-goal matching success is at least 60 percent in every checkpoint,
- counterfactual-goal matching success is at least 25 percent task-macro and at least 20 percent
  in every checkpoint,
- each directional pooled paired advantage is at least 20 percentage points,
- the pooled symmetric aligned-prompt advantage is at least 20 percentage points,
- both directional advantages and the symmetric advantage are strictly positive in every
  checkpoint,
- one-sided exact McNemar p-values are at most 0.01 for both directional comparisons,
- one-sided exact sign-flip p-values over ten task sums, each retaining all checkpoints, episodes,
  and targets together, are at most 0.01 for both directional comparisons and the symmetric
  interaction,
- for each directional comparison and the symmetric interaction, at least eight of ten pooled task
  gaps are strictly positive and no task gap is negative.

Every task, target swap, checkpoint, and raw paired count is reported whether or not the gate
passes. Pooled McNemar tests are exact over the 1,500 paired four-arm units but are not
cluster-robust. The task-level sign-flip tests retain all repeated outcomes together and are exact
conditional on the ten observed task-sum magnitudes. These are finite-protocol paired tests, not
population confidence intervals. Task and checkpoint consistency protect against a pooled result
driven by a small subset.

## Claim boundary

A pass supports counterfactual goal following for familiar vocabulary, familiar objects, familiar
Object-suite scenes, synthetic target-only BDDL goal rewrites, and the three fixed checkpoints. It
does not establish paraphrase robustness, policy-unseen or unseen-object generalization, novel
composition, cross-suite transfer, physical-robot transfer, or a benefit unique to tensor
decomposability. Rank 96 was fixed before corrected projector outcomes, but the earlier rank screen
was invalidated. Tasks 8 and 9 were absent from corrected projector construction and used during
policy training, so they are neither a clean rank-selection holdout nor policy-unseen.
