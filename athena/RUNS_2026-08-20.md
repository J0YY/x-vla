The A6000 replication measures a 3.49-times eager gap and a 1.73-times compiled gap. Compiler
optimization therefore leaves a consistent 1.73 to 1.74-times batch-one latency ratio across the
two tested GPU classes.

The surgery sweep crosses joint blocks `{0,2,4,6,7}` with ranks `{64,128,256}` on discovery tasks
`[0,4)`. Each configuration compares baseline, keep-top, Frobenius-matched keep-random,
remove-top, and Frobenius-matched remove-random. The advance gate is at least 15 percentage points
in both prespecified directions, followed by held-out task confirmation.

All fifteen configurations completed. None passed both gates. The best minimum advantage is block 0,
rank 128: keep-top beats matched random by 10 points, while removing the selected directions is 40
points more damaging than matched random removal. Because the keep margin misses the 15-point gate,
there is no held-out confirmation candidate and no successful selective coefficient edit.

The all-layer audit reconstructs four vision and eight joint attention modules per checkpoint,
or 384 heads across three checkpoints. Worst relative $\ell_2$ error is $2.56\times10^{-7}$.
Two checkpoints meet the original $2\times10^{-5}$ absolute indicator. Seed 2 reaches
$2.23\times10^{-5}$ absolute error, and that miss is retained rather than rounded away.

Across the three rational checkpoints, renaming the target shifts relative end-effector
preference by 0.168 to 0.189 m and moves the trajectory toward the renamed object in 84 to 88
percent of 100 paired rollouts. Only 28 to 33 percent end closer to it. The conventional seed-0
twin has a larger 0.239 m shift, a 92 percent directional response, and a 33 percent endpoint
rate. The grounding response is therefore not unique to decomposability.

The visual-subspace replication rebuilds a rank-64 gripper-sensitive Gram independently for each
checkpoint, then evaluates full, selected, and fixed-random projections over four tasks and five
matched canonical trials per task. The cross-suite runs are explicitly zero-shot controls for the
Object-trained checkpoints, not a substitute for multi-suite training.

The partial-conversion controls separate bilinear attention and FFNs with ordinary per-token RMS
normalization from softmax attention and SwiGLU with rational normalization. The offline diagnostics
use the same fixed 4,096 cache samples for every checkpoint to distinguish checkpoint corruption or
poor behavior-cloning fit from closed-loop covariate-shift failure.

The task-3 replication reverses the seed-0 comparison. χ seeds 1 and 2 each reach $41/50$, while
the corresponding conventional checkpoints reach $37/50$ and $33/50$. Task 3 is therefore a
checkpoint-specific gap rather than a consistent cost of the rational architecture. Full-suite χ
evaluations remain necessary because both new conventional checkpoints fail most other tasks.
