## CP-term compression pilot prepared 2026-08-21

`launch_cp_pruning.sh` preregisters a weight-only structured-compression test on the fixed χ seed-2
checkpoint, which reaches 450/500 before pruning. Each bilinear FFN component is scored by the
product of its homogeneous left-row norm, homogeneous right-row norm, and down-column norm. This
score is invariant to the usual reciprocal rescaling of CP factors. The lowest-scoring 25, 50, 60,
67, and 75 percent of terms are removed from every bilinear FFN and compared with three independently
sampled matched random masks at each fraction.

The pilot uses all ten Object tasks and the first five canonical states per task. The prespecified
positive gate is that magnitude pruning retains at least 90 percent of the unpruned successes and
exceeds the median matched-random success rate by at least 15 percentage points at one removal
fraction. All five fractions and all fifteen random controls are reported. A passing fraction is then
confirmed on all 500 canonical trials and the other two χ checkpoints. This is a structured FFN
compression claim, not whole-model parameter compression, until compact modules are materialized
and timed.

The smoke is job 830709. The original 25, 50, and 75 percent pilots are jobs 830710 to 830721.
Before any pilot outcome was available, the 60 and 67 percent transition points were added as jobs
830722 to 830729. At 50 percent CP-term removal, the equivalent removable count is 5,983,488
parameters, or 29.7 percent of the complete policy.

The 25 percent result completed at 41/50, compared with 44/50 for the unpruned checkpoint on the
same canonical states. Three random masks reach 38/50, 36/50, and 41/50. This passes the 90 percent
retention condition but not the 15-point selectivity gate. While the 50 percent magnitude run was
still active, its first eight tasks showed a sharp collapse. The transition search was therefore
adaptively expanded to 30, 35, 40, and 45 percent as jobs 830746 to 830761. These new points are
exploratory discovery. Any chosen fraction still requires the prespecified blind confirmation.

Three fixed convolutional checkpoints were copied from the project volume to Athena and verified
byte-for-byte by SHA-256. The strongest one has a previously completed 472/500 evaluation.
`launch_conv_cp_pruning.sh` adds an Athena-native 50-state baseline plus a 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, and
50 percent magnitude-pruning curve. This is an architecture-replication screen. Matched random
controls will be run only at a selected fraction, followed by all-state confirmation.

The same verified convolutional checkpoints are queued for an independent ensemble replication.
Arithmetic mean is the primary outcome because these checkpoints previously reached 481/500 by
mean aggregation under the matched Modal protocol. Coordinate median is a prespecified secondary
outcome. Both reductions use four Athena shards totaling 500 canonical trials, and every task is
reported. This test is confirmatory rather than a new model-selection search.

An exploratory corrected-ensemble test uses three previously produced low-dosage corrective

## Fixed-observation local instruction specificity

The initial VLM4RWD language intervention jobs are excluded before execution. A code audit found
that their control construction assumed exactly two visible object bodies, while canonical Object
scenes contain six. Smoke 831002, full jobs 831003 through 831005, and summary 831006 were canceled
before start. They produced no scientific outcomes and consumed no GPU time. Provenance job 830988

The corrected version is a prospectively frozen local follow-up before its eight-action outcomes,
not an independent preregistration. Earlier valid 80-step interventions on canonical episodes zero
through nine showed a positive language effect and motivated this test. Those states are used only
for the ten-state technical smoke. The primary set is canonical episodes 10 through 49 for every
task, giving 400 untouched states per checkpoint and 1,200 primary state-checkpoint trials.

At each state, one model input is used to predict eight-action chunks for all ten official Object
prompts and an empty, BOS-only language control. Each fixed chunk is executed open loop after a
deterministic reset. The post-settle full MuJoCo state, model image, robot state, end effector, and
all six grocery positions must hash identically for all eleven conditions. Every result records
checkpoint, cache, provenance, metadata, runner, and imported-model source hashes, together with
prompt text and token IDs, episode identities, all action chunks, and all clone hashes. Reward and
BDDL success are not experimental outcomes.

For target A and each of the five co-present distractors B, the local score is one half of the dot
product between their paired end-effector displacement difference and their starting unit-direction
difference. On the same A-versus-B axis, its specificity rank is computed against all 12 ordered
contrasts among the four prompts whose groceries are truly absent. The five scores and five ranks
are averaged to one state-level score and rank before any task aggregation. Distractors are ordered
by official prompt identifier, and no prompt, object, task, state, or control is selected from an
outcome.

A checkpoint passes only if its 20,000-draw empirical hierarchical stability interval for mean
score has a lower endpoint above zero, at least eight of ten task means are positive, mean
specificity rank is at least 0.75, and its rank stability interval has a lower endpoint above 0.50.
All three fixed checkpoints must pass without changing a threshold. These are resampling intervals
over the finite canonical task-state set, not population confidence intervals.

A pass supports only a replicated differential eight-action displacement along the five
target-versus-distractor axes, larger than typical contrasts among the four absent-object prompts.
It does not establish absolute motion toward either named object, bidirectional noun selection,
counterfactual task success, robust grounding, zero-shot language understanding, broad-suite
generalization, a practically meaningful effect size, or a benefit unique to tensor decomposability.
